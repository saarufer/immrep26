What structure did and did not contribute

All 2,636 complexes were folded twice, plus five diffusion samples per complex and 900 extra folds against decoy peptides — about 30 GPU-hours. Structure is the weakest of our four model terms. This records why, and what that does and does not mean.

Setup. Boltz-2; inputs are the TCR α/β variable domains, the peptide and a reduced class-I α1/α2 platform, with empty MSAs. Two complete independent runs of all 2,636 complexes, five diffusion samples per complex in one run, and 900 additional folds of 300 TCRs against anchor-matched decoy peptides. All figures are mean per-peptide ROC-AUC on the five public peptides unless stated otherwise.

1Structural confidence does not encode peptide specificity

The best standalone structural scalar reaches 0.757, against 0.851 for pooled k-NN sequence density. The organisers' own AlphaFold3 min-PAE baseline reached 0.787 — below their tcrdist3 baseline at 0.825. Two results explain why.

  • An interface containing no TCR at all scores 0.647. iptm_mhc_pep, peptide against MHC only, predicts whether a TCR record validates. Whatever the confidence measures, it is not the TCR–peptide contact.
  • Differencing against matched decoys collapses the signal to chance. We re-folded 300 TCRs (30 positive and 30 negative per public peptide) against three anchor-matched decoy peptides each — HLA-A*02:01 constrains an aliphatic P2 and C-terminus, A*01:01 an acidic P3 and aromatic C-terminus. Target-only confidence predicts validation at 0.63–0.74. Subtracting the decoy, which cancels everything that is a property of the TCR alone, leaves a margin of +0.0061 against a decoy-choice noise of 0.036. Every margin is null.

Structural confidence is a property of the receptor and its fold, not of the pairing it was folded in.

2The mechanism: the same interface is built for every peptide

Six of the eight peptides are HLA-A*02:01 nonamers, so HLA and peptide length are held fixed and anything remaining is peptide-specific. Across six geometric spread measures, the hardest cohort — GLCTLVAML, where sequence density alone reaches only 0.677 — ranks 4.3 of 6, which is unexceptional, and its crossing-angle spread of 15.13° is the second highest. The median per-position contact footprint is nearly identical across all six. The folding model cannot resolve peptide specificity in these data.

3Structure still contributes — as a receptor-quality prior

Added to a single sequence-density term, structure is worth +0.0102 (0.8396 → 0.8498), and in the submitted four-term ensemble it is the weakest member. The contribution is real and partly independent, but it answers “is this a canonical, well-formed receptor?” rather than “does it bind this peptide?”. A database record created by chain mispairing or barcode swapping folds badly for reasons that have nothing to do with antigen.

4The one genuinely new axis: a coordinate system, not a score

Every feature above is a scalar summarising one interface. The 60-dimensional contact fingerprint is instead indexed by peptide position, and within a cohort the peptide is constant, so two TCRs can be compared by where they touch the antigen. Not “how confident is this interface” but “do TCRs that engage the peptide the same way validate the same way”.

This is the only structural object that is genuinely decorrelated from the rest of the model:

5Standalone AUC does not predict incremental value — the ranking inverts

Residualising interface-energetics features on the full model reverses their order completely.

The features that look strongest are re-encoding sequence density in structural clothing. The one that looks like noise is orthogonal — ρ +0.002 with receptor typicality — and survives.

Screen structural features on residual-after-model, never on standalone AUC. This is the most transferable thing we learned.

6Prediction noise is not the limiting factor

Two complete independent Boltz runs make the noise decomposition direct. On 25 sampled complexes the TCR Cα RMSD after superposing on MHC and peptide has median 1.19 Å and reaches 5.76 Å; none are identical.

Interface polar fraction, defined and argued for entirely on one run, recomputes from the other at 0.5490 / +0.0021 — within noise of the original. This is the project's only out-of-sample structural confirmation, and it involves no selection. Pooled between-run Spearman is +0.856 (0.747–0.895 per peptide), giving a single-run reliability of 0.812: 81% of the feature's variance is reproducible signal, 19% prediction noise.

Disattenuating the residual by that reliability gives the headroom:

A noise-free structure predictor would move this feature from 0.5586 to about 0.5650 — roughly 0.006 of AUC, a tenth of its whole margin over chance. Better structures would not change any conclusion here. The ceiling is informational, not technical.

One exception worth flagging: CINGVCWTV's reliability is 0.683, the lowest of the eight and the only private peptide among them.

Averaging five models makes the measurement more reliable and the prediction slightly worse. That is the signature of an information ceiling, not a noise ceiling.

8Germline docking polarity is real but does not transfer

Computed from the predicted structures directly — 5 Å heavy-atom contacts, MHC α1 = residues 57–84, α2 = 138–176 — the canonical diagonal, α chain over α2 and β over α1, holds for most complexes. But 7–36% dock non-canonically, and NLVPMVATV is a marked outlier at only 64% canonical against 81–93% elsewhere. The fingerprint machinery itself was validated against solved complexes and is faithful. The polarity varies within every cohort, and that variation carried nothing after residualising on the model.

9Scope, and the strongest objection

Everything above is measured on one setup: Boltz-2, empty MSAs, a reduced class-I platform. We did not run an AlphaFold3 control, so we cannot separate “structure carries little here” from “this modelling setup carries little here”.

IMMREP25 — a genuinely different task, unseen-peptide specificity prediction, where no same-cohort neighbours exist — found that structural modelling was what distinguished the top methods. We do not read our results as contradicting that. The reconciliation we favour is that structure did not get weaker: neighbourhood density on a populated cohort is simply much stronger here, and it leaves structure only the residual.
